Objectives

Pasquevich, Dreon, Diupotex-Chong and Heras investigate a novel family of hyperstable apple snail egg proteins (Perivitellin-1, PV1) from Pomacea snails, asking how protein stability relates to functional evolution. Their subject is PpaPV1 from the most basal lineage, Flagellata, which p.1 reports displays unparalleled structural and kinetic stability, ranking among the most hyperstable proteins documented in nature.

Importance to Field

The relationship between stability and functional evolution is, as the abstract on p.1 puts it, little explored for proteins purified from natural sources, so this is a rare natural dataset rather than an engineered one. p.10 develops the evolutionary argument that high stability may have provided tolerance to mutations, allowing new functions to be acquired during Pomacea diversification without compromising the native structure.

How and Why Your Work Was Used

The glycan-binding side of the study is where the applicant's tool enters. In Materials and Methods on p.4 the authors write that "The results of glycan specificity for PpaPV1 were compared with those of PsSC (Ituarte et al., 2018) and PdPV1 (Brola et al., 2020) using the Glycan Array Dashboard (glycotoolkit.com/GLAD/) (Mehta and Cummings, 2019)". The tool is named by full name, by URL, and by the applicant's author citation, and its role is comparison: it is how the lectin specificity of this basal protein was set against the previously published specificities of related proteins from other clades. That comparison is what supports the abstract's claim on p.1 that PpaPV1 has a milder lectin activity with narrower specificity than PV1s from the closely related Bridgesii clade. This folder also holds the earlier version of the work, which matters because the date of the statement relative to the applicant's petition date is what the extra files were collected to establish.

Findings / Conclusions / Recommendation

The paper concludes on p.1 that there is evidence for substantial structural and functional changes throughout the genus's PV1 evolution, with structural and kinetic stability decreasing in a clade-related fashion alongside large variation in defensive traits — the pale PpaPV1's lectin turning potent in the Bridgesii clade, adversely affecting gut morphology. p.10 frames this as stability enabling functional evolution.

Objectives

Li, Peralta, Schoffelen, Lewis and colleagues present LeGenD, a method for high-throughput N-glycan profiling using explainable artificial intelligence combined with lectin profiling. The abstract on p.1 frames the objective as increasing the throughput and interpretability of glycan profiling, and the workflow figure on p.4 sets out the curated training set, the glycoform and glycoprotein outputs, and the lectin-derived features the model learns from.

Importance to Field

The authors make the field-level case themselves in their summary on p.8: "as awareness of the importance of glycosylation increases, new technologies are needed to increase the accessibility and throughput of glycan research tools". They also report on p.8 that SHAP analysis enhances understanding of the data beyond the raw weights of the neural network, which addresses the interpretability objection that usually limits machine-learning methods in this area.

How and Why Your Work Was Used

The Materials and Methods on p.12 states that "The glycan structures were drawn using GlycoGlyph (112)", with the tool's URL given, reference 112 on p.15 being the applicant's GlycoGlyph paper. In a paper whose contribution is a computational pipeline for glycan profiling, the applicant's application is the named source of the glycan structure representations — the visual vocabulary in which the method's results are communicated. It is a Methods sentence in a paper building glycomics tooling, which is the most directly relevant kind of venue for this citation.

Findings / Conclusions / Recommendation

The paper reports that LeGenD performs high-throughput N-glycan profiling with explainable AI, and its summary on p.8 is that the explainability layer (SHAP) adds understanding beyond the network's weights. Its recommendation, also on p.8, is that the field needs more accessible and higher-throughput glycan tools — the argument the applicant's own tools were built to answer.

Objectives

Zhang, Kotopanova, Dang and colleagues publish in Nucleic Acids Research a Methods article introducing AquIRE, an assay for clinically induced RNA damage, which they then apply to the conservation and dynamics of glycoRNAs. The abstract on p.1 sets both halves: the damage-detection method, and what applying it reveals about glycoRNAs.

Importance to Field

GlycoRNAs are a contested and technically difficult subject, and the value of a new assay here is that it can detect species that earlier methods missed. The authors' own summary on p.12 is that "using the sensitivity of our AquIRE assay in conjugation with lectins allows us to detect glycoRNAs that display similar molecular biology to those described previously", which is a claim about both the assay and the biology.

How and Why Your Work Was Used

On p.5, in the Materials and Methods describing how the glycan-related figures were made, the authors state simply that "Glycans were drawn using GlycoGlyph [30]", reference 30 on p.17 being the applicant's GlycoGlyph paper. The sentence is short and unambiguous: the glycan structures that appear in this Nucleic Acids Research Methods paper were produced with the applicant's application rather than drawn by hand or with another package. GlycoGlyph's purpose is exactly this — drawing and naming glycan structures — so a Methods sentence assigning that task to it is the tool doing the job it was published to do.

Findings / Conclusions / Recommendation

The paper reports the mechanisms by which clinically induced RNA damage can be detected and applies them to glycoRNA conservation, concluding on p.12 that the lectin-combined assay detects glycoRNAs with molecular biology consistent with previously described ones.

Objectives

Petralia, van Diepen, Foster, Hokke and colleagues set out to characterise the glycan repertoire of Schistosoma haematobium, the species responsible for most human schistosome infection but, as p.1 puts it, largely unstudied compared with S. mansoni, and to ask whether the two species differ in ways that matter immunologically and diagnostically.

Importance to Field

The stated gap is clinically meaningful: S. haematobium causes the majority of infections and its own associated pathology, yet the in-depth glycome work had been done on a different species. p.1 reports that they found substantial differences from S. mansoni in core structure, fucosylation patterns and glucuronic acid modifications of glycosphingolipid glycans, which means the widely transferred assumptions from S. mansoni do not carry over.

How and Why Your Work Was Used

The article's Materials and Methods describes the glycan array work, and on p.16 the authors write that "Finally, GLycan Array Dashboard (GLAD) 93, version 3.1, from the glycotoolkit website (glycotoolkit.com/Tools/GLAD/) hosted by the National Center for Functional Glycomics, was used for data visualization, data mining, generation of heatmaps and boxplot graphs". The named tool is the applicant's, cited by reference 93 (the GLAD Bioinformatics paper, listed on p.19), and the sentence assigns it four specific analytical jobs — visualisation, data mining, and the two graph types in which the array results are presented.

Findings / Conclusions / Recommendation

The authors conclude on p.1 that S. haematobium and S. mansoni are differentially glycosylated and that S. haematobium glycans are involved in parasite-host immunobiology, reporting that IgG from S. haematobium-infected individuals preferentially binds the acidic GSL glycans relative to IgG from S. mansoni-infected individuals. They identify specific, immunogenic S. haematobium glycan targets, which is the diagnostic implication stated in the abstract.

Objectives

Nuschy and Wilson, writing in the Journal of Biological Chemistry, set out to extend their own defined glycan microarrays to a library of natural anionic structures — N-acetylneuraminic acid and N-glycolylneuraminic acid, plus sulfated galactose, N-acetylglucosamine and glucuronic acid — built with recombinant glycosyl- and sulfo-transferases, and to compare anionic modifications on linear tetrasaccharides against typical biantennary N-glycan cores. p.2 states the goal: to analyse biantennary and linear glycans for accessibility and minimal requirements for lectin binding, and to re-examine anionic-epitope-recognising lectins and antibodies across different sialic acid types.

Importance to Field

The paper's opening on p.1 makes the case that this work is still needed: despite many studies of commercial lectin binding patterns, "unexpected recognition motifs are still revealed", and these reagents are used for rapid screening of glycosylation patterns and for diagnostic histochemistry. So a specificity that turns out narrower than assumed has consequences for anyone reading a diagnostic slide with that lectin.

How and Why Your Work Was Used

GLAD is named twice, in the two places a reader would look. In the figure captions on p.4 and repeated on p.5, the authors state that "The heat map was generated using the GLAD tool and represents the relative fluorescent units (RFU) normalized to 70,000 as the maximum value" — so the heat-map images that carry the binding results are output of the applicant's software. And in Materials and Methods, under "Microarray printing, analysis, and data processing" on p.10, they write that the data "are depicted as heat-maps (Figs. 3-5, summarized in Fig. S6) and graphs" and that "the former generated using the GLAD tool (59) representing the relative fluorescent units (RFU) normalized to 70,000 as the maximum value", reference 59 being the GLAD paper listed on p.12. This is the tool producing the reported figures, described in the methods section with a formal citation, and the same normalisation the authors chose is the one GLAD applies.

Findings / Conclusions / Recommendation

The authors report on p.6 that they "conclude that sulfate is not required for efficient antibody binding on a microarray, but rather the β4-linkage of the Gal to which the glucuronic acid is attached is important", and their summary on p.6 is that for most lectins and glycan-binding proteins there were no severe differences between biantennary N-glycans, Lewis-type glycans and linear saccharides.

Objectives

Jamieson, Shen, Abu-Shmais and Georgiev and colleagues isolate glycan-reactive antibodies from human HIV-1 vaccine trial participants and characterise their pathogen cross-reactivity. p.1 states the motivation: understanding the B-cell repertoire that vaccination can engage in humans is crucial for developing preventive vaccines, and the study works from peripheral blood mononuclear cells of trial participants.

Importance to Field

The glycan shield of HIV-1 has been a long-standing obstacle to antibody-based vaccine design. The authors' proposal, on p.3, inverts it: "we propose that the elicitation of glycan-reactive antibodies can serve as an effective strategy for turning the elusive HIV-1 glycan shield into a Trojan horse", and they find on p.7 that although the antibodies are autoreactive, their overall reactivity toward self-antigen is limited, which is what makes the class safe to pursue. That safety finding is a prerequisite for the vaccine strategy, not an afterthought.

How and Why Your Work Was Used

Both of the applicant's contributions appear in this paper's array work, in the Methods and in a figure legend. Objectives

Sombutkayasith, Watthammawut, Guérardel and colleagues set out to establish whether the infectious hypodermal and hematopoietic necrosis virus (IHHNV), which damages farmed Penaeus vannamei shrimp, attaches to host tissues through glycans. Their account of the work runs from the title and summary on p.1 through the results beginning on p.6, where they probe viral capsid and capsid-protein binding to glycan classes, and they conclude on p.3 that high-mannose N-glycans and fucosylated O-glycans act as primary attachment factors.

Importance to Field

Shrimp viral disease is an agricultural problem of real scale, and the paper's own framing on p.1 is that the entry route of IHHNV into host tissue was not understood. Establishing a glycan dependence gives the field a receptor-level hypothesis to test and, as the authors put it on p.3, points to glycan-mediated interaction as a target for intervention rather than leaving the virus's host range unexplained.

How and Why Your Work Was Used

The article's glycan microarray work is described under the heading "Glycan microarray" on p.29, where the authors state that binding specificity of IHHNV virus-like particles was evaluated on the National Center for Functional Glycomics printed glycan microarray platform (version 5.6) with 539 glycans in six replicates, citing the applicant's review of the platform and its glycan-interactome use (Heimburg-Molinaro, Mehta, Tilton and Cummings) as the source of the method. On p.30 they write that signal intensities "were processed using the MAWE workflow, which excludes the highest and lowest replicate values before calculating the mean relative fluorescence units (RFU) standard deviation, and coefficient of variation for each glycan (Mehta et al. 2024)" — that is, the applicant's published data-reduction rules for reusable arrays governed how every value in their figures was computed. The same paragraph on p.30 continues that the results were "analyzed with GLAD (Glycan Array Dashboard), which enables comparison of binding profiles and identification of enriched glycan motifs (Mehta and Cummings 2019)", so GLAD was the tool through which the binding profiles were compared and the enriched motifs identified. Both instruments are named in Materials and Methods, in the paragraph that produces the paper's glycan-binding evidence, and the applicant's personal name appears in the in-text citation on both.

Findings / Conclusions / Recommendation

The authors report that the viral particles bind the glycan classes they expected for an attachment step, and on p.8 they state that the results "collectively strongly support the presence of complex glycans harboring terminal mannose, GlcNAc/GalNAc, fucose residues" on the host side. Their closing inference on p.3 is that high-mannose N-glycans and fucosylated O-glycans serve as primary attachment factors in IHHNV infection.
