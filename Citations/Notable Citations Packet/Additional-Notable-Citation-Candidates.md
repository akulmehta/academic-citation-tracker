## Group A: strongest (independent work built on, or depends on, your finding)

### A1. cit-000044: Konteatis et al., Agios, ACS Med Chem Lett (published Jan 22, 2020)
*Vorasidenib (AG-881): a first-in-class, brain-penetrant dual inhibitor of mutant IDH1 and 2 for treatment of glioma.*
- **Your work cited:** Verespy et al. 2016, Sci Rep, "Allosteric partial inhibition of monomeric proteases" (Project 1).
- **Quote (Results, p.2):** "Both enasidenib and AGI-12026 show partial inhibition of the IDH1-R132H homodimer (Table 1) as allosteric modulators have been known to induce both full and intermediate structural changes in the active site of enzymes resulting in varying degrees of efficacy."
- **Why it matters:** an industry drug-discovery team (a pharmaceutical company, not an academic lab) uses your allosteric partial-inhibition concept to explain the behavior of a cancer drug candidate. It shows the concept travelled from thrombin to a different enzyme and to a clinical program.
- **Proposed Section I:** Project 1; type: industry (company) research article; tier B. **Section II:** *Objectives:* explain why two inhibitors only partly block mutant IDH1. *How and why used:* the authors invoked your finding that allosteric modulators can give an intermediate (partial) rather than full block. *Findings:* their data fit that pattern.
- **Caveat:** only state vorasidenib's later regulatory approval after verifying it separately.

### A2. cit-000110: Chen, ..., Weitz, Biochemistry 2017 (McMaster)
*Exosite 2-directed ligands attenuate protein C activation by the thrombin–thrombomodulin complex.*
- **Your work cited:** "Allosterism-based simultaneous, dual anticoagulant and antiplatelet action" (2016) (Project 1).
- **Quote (Discussion, p.8):** "In support of this concept, a synthetic sulfated lignin that binds exosite 2 not only modulated thrombin activity in vitro but also attenuated thrombosis without increasing the extent of bleeding in mouse thrombosis and bleeding models. Therefore, our findings point to new avenues by which to modulate thrombin activity in thrombosis and hemostasis."
- **Why it matters:** one of the leading thrombosis groups cites your in-vivo result as the *evidence* for the concept their own study supports.
- **Proposed:** Project 1; tier C (Discussion); Section II *Importance to the Field* and *How and why used* (your result was used as supporting evidence for their central concept).

### A3. cit-001023: Verhamme (with Bock lab), Analytical Biochemistry 2012 (Vanderbilt)
*Fluorescent reporters of thrombin, heparin cofactor II, and heparin binding in a ternary complex.*
