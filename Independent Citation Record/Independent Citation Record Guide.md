Independent Citation Record Guide 
To make an independent citation record, go to your Google Scholar profile. For each publication, 
click on the number of its citations. This will take you to a listing of the citations to the 
publication. There may be more than one page of citations for some of your publications. You 
should make PDFs of these pages. On these pages, you should then highlight the title of each 
independent citation (citations where the citing paper and cited paper share no authors). You 
should make sure that on each citation page, the title of your article is posted at the top of the 
page.  
For your convenience, we have uploaded a sample independent citation page to Section II of the 
I-140 RFE tab (“Sample Independent Citation Page.pdf”). For our ease of access, please 
either consolidate all your citing article lists into a single PDF file, or please upload these PDFs 
together in an organized .zip folder. Please also include a copy of the Google Scholar profile 
page displaying your total number of citations at the time of preparing the independent 
citation record.  
For any independent citations that are not listed in English, we should provide a certified 
English translation (i.e., an English translation and an accompanying translation affidavit). The 
translation of each entry should list the title of the article, the list of authors, and the venue in 
which the article was published. You are welcome to complete these translations yourself or 
get in touch with an individual(s) fluent in both English and the original language to translate the 
citation(s) on your behalf. If you or a colleague, acquaintance, friend, or family member do the 
certified translation yourselves, please complete the template we have uploaded to Section II of 
the I-140 RFE tab (“Translation_Affidavit.docx”). Then, please sign and date it. Otherwise, 
please have an official translation service complete a certified translation for you.  
As an important disclaimer, USCIS does not consider translations conducted by automated 
translation tools like Google Translate to be “certified.” Rather, only the translator himself or 
herself – a human individual fluent in both the original and translated languages – can 
certify the accuracy of his or her own translation. Neither Google Translate itself nor a Google 
Translate user can certify an automated translation. Therefore, you should not perform your own 
translations using Google Translate. Thank you for understanding.  
If you do not wish to obtain certified English translations of the non-English citation entries in 
your independent citation record, you should refrain from highlighting the non-English citation 
entries in your citation record and provide us with an adjusted independent citation total and 
percentage based solely on your English citations. To calculate this adjusted independent 
citation percentage, you would want to divide your total number of independent citations in 
English by the total number of English citations indexed on your Google Scholar profile.  
In addition, you are welcome to create a pie chart or similar visual aid depicting the breakdown 
between your independent and dependent citation percentages and totals. If you do this, we 
recommend dating or timestamping the chart so that it does not need to be continuously 
updated every time your citations increase. That said, this chart can certainly be updated shortly 
before we finalize your RFE response, especially if your independent citation percentage 
improves between now and your final deadline.
